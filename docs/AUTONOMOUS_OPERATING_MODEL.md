# Autonomous Operating Model

Veridara AI is designed to move from an agent demo toward a durable operating company. The control loop is:

`Observe → Decide → Govern → Execute → Measure → Reconcile → Learn → Reinvest`

## 1. Revenue-first operation

The company should continuously search for real demand through:

- B2B service opportunities and proposals
- commercial partnerships and sponsorships
- affiliate product opportunities
- media and creator commerce
- repeat customers and account expansion

A revenue target is a planning constraint, not a guarantee. Revenue counts as business output only when supported by a real customer/order/conversion, delivery evidence, and—where applicable—provider or payment reconciliation.

## 2. Workforce and agents

Every agent should have:

- a stable identity and role
- explicit capabilities
- scoped permissions
- a business owner
- auditable actions
- measurable outcomes
- escalation rules
- periodic evaluation and maintenance

Agents may coordinate work across functions, but financial, customer, security, legal, and other material side effects remain governed by deterministic controls and the required human approvals.

## 3. Operating departments

The target operating model is intentionally simple:

| Function | Primary outcome |
| --- | --- |
| CEO / Strategy | Direction, priorities, partnerships |
| Finance | Cash, accounting, runway, controls |
| Sales | Qualified pipeline, proposals, closed revenue |
| Marketing / Growth | Demand, audience, acquisition |
| Content / Media | Useful content, distribution, monetization |
| Product / Engineering | Reliable systems and automation |
| Operations | Delivery, quality, cycle time |
| Customer Success | Retention, satisfaction, expansion |
| People / HR | Workforce capability, learning, responsible management |

The repository already has pieces of this model. The remaining work is to connect them into one measurable business loop instead of adding agents for their own sake.

## 4. Management rhythm

### Every cycle
- inspect company state
- run agents
- validate proposals through the Governor
- execute only allowed actions
- persist receipts and audit evidence
- publish operational events through the outbox

### Every day
- review failures and blocked actions
- inspect customer and commercial pipeline
- verify important external results
- identify bottlenecks
- prioritize revenue-producing work

### Every month
- close accounting
- reconcile revenue and payouts
- settle payroll obligations according to configured policy
- review business-unit economics
- review agent reliability and business impact
- revise targets, budgets and experiments

## 5. Quality standard

An agent is not considered effective because it produces many tasks. It must demonstrate:

1. correctness
2. reliability
3. useful output
4. measurable business impact
5. controlled cost
6. traceability
7. safe escalation when outside authority

This matches current enterprise guidance that production agents need measurable operational and business outcomes, verified identity, scoped access, auditable records, continuous monitoring and clear human accountability. See the cited references in the project audit.

## 6. What “self-operating” means here

The intended architecture is autonomous execution inside explicit boundaries, not unrestricted autonomy.

The company can continuously:

- observe its economic and operational state
- identify opportunities
- assign work to agents
- run experiments
- create commercial artifacts
- track customers and receivables
- reconcile verified revenue
- learn from outcomes
- recommend reinvestment

External platform publishing, outbound communications, payment initiation, identity/RBAC and portfolio capital allocation remain explicit production gates until their adapters and acceptance evidence are complete.

## 7. Non-goals

Do not optimize for:

- number of agents
- number of employees
- cosmetic dashboard complexity
- unverified revenue
- simulated external activity
- autonomous employment decisions

Optimize for durable outcomes: customers, delivery, verified revenue, cash discipline, reliability, reputation and learning.
